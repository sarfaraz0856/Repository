Part II — The Morning After. Pripyat, 26–27 April 1986.

The sun rose over a city that had no idea. Three kilometres away, the ruin of Reactor No. 4 was still burning, sending a column of radioactive smoke north-west across Europe.

It was Saturday. Children went to school, couples got married, and families walked under the spring trees.

On 27 April, helicopters began dropping sand, boron and lead into the open reactor. At 13:10 the loudspeakers announced the evacuation. Residents were told to bring their documents and enough for three days.

Around 1,200 buses carried 49,360 people out of Pripyat in about three and a half hours.

They never returned.
