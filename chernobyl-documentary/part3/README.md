## Historical notes

- **Helicopter drops:** from 27 April to about 10 May, helicopter crews dropped around 5,000 tonnes of sand, clay, dolomite, boron carbide and lead into the reactor.
- **Vehicles:** buses, fire engines, APCs and helicopters that had worked in the zone were too contaminated to be used again. Many were left at sites such as Rassokha and Buriakivka.
- **The roof:** in September 1986, after remote-controlled robots failed in the radiation, about 3,800 soldiers and reservists cleared highly radioactive graphite and fuel debris from the roofs of Unit 3 and the vent-stack block by hand. Each man was allowed from about 40 to 90 seconds. The real footage is covered in white flecks where radiation hit the film, and the shot imitates this.
- **The sarcophagus** (the "Shelter" object) was completed on 30 November 1986, about 206 days after the accident.
- **Liquidators:** about 600,000 people took part in the clean-up between 1986 and 1990.
- **Pripyat** lies inside the exclusion zone and has not been inhabited since the evacuation.

## Sound

- **Music:** a low pedal and dark strings under the helicopter, sparse strings over the field of machines, a dissonant tremolo cluster with a heartbeat drum on the roof, a swell at the sarcophagus, then piano and high strings in the snow.
- **Effects:** the Mi-8 and the impact of its load; crows, wind and metal creaks in the field; boots on rubble, breathing through respirators, shovels, falling blocks and a dense Geiger counter on the roof; crane diesels and distant clangs at dusk; winter wind and faint Geiger clicks in the snow.
