If your video is only in Hindi, it mostly reaches people who understand Hindi.
But if the same video can also be heard in English, new viewers around the world can watch it.
And you don't need to upload a second video for this.
With YouTube's multi-language audio feature, you can add an audio track in another language to the same video.
Today I'll show you, step by step, how to dub your YouTube video in English.
From translating the script, to making the English voice, syncing the audio, and uploading it in YouTube Studio.
I just did this whole process for one of my own videos, so I'll show you the practical way.
First, let's understand what multi-language audio is.
Your video stays the same. Same link, same views, same comments.
It just has more than one audio track inside, like one in Hindi and one in English.
Viewers click the gear icon in the player, and choose their language under Audio track.
And often, YouTube plays the right track automatically, based on the viewer's language.
The benefit is that one video can reach viewers in many different countries.
Now, one important thing. This feature isn't on every channel yet.
YouTube is rolling it out gradually, to creators who have access to advanced features.
To check, open Settings in YouTube Studio, then Channel, then Feature eligibility.
See whether advanced features are on. If needed, start with phone verification.
If you still don't see the option to add an audio track, the feature hasn't reached your channel yet.
Even then, you can still add an English title, description and subtitles.
So how do you make the dub? There are two ways.
The first is YouTube's automatic dubbing, where YouTube itself creates the translated audio for you.
It's available in twenty-seven languages, and those videos are labelled auto-dubbed.
It's easy, but you don't get full control over the voice and translation.
The second way is making your own dub, where you decide the script, the voice and the timing.
Today we'll learn this second way, because it gives you full control over quality.
Remember, if a language already has an auto dub, you need to delete it before uploading your own.
Step one, translate your script into English.
The easiest way is to give your Hindi script or subtitle file to Claude, or another AI tool.
And ask it for a natural English translation of every line, not word by word.
Here's a very important trick. Ask it to keep the English lines short and clear.
English often runs longer than Hindi, and then it won't fit in the time of the Hindi line.
So give the timing with every line, so the translation is written for that duration.
Once you get the translation, read it yourself, and check technical words, names and numbers.
The better the translation, the more natural the dub will sound.
Step two, get the English voice ready.
If your English is good, the best option is to record it in your own voice.
Another option is a text-to-speech tool, where you paste the English script and get a voiceover.
Some tools can also clone your own voice, so the English dub still sounds like you.
But only clone your own voice, or someone else's with their permission.
Make a separate file for each line, so if there's a mistake, you only redo that line.
Then listen to every line carefully. Check for wrong or repeated words, or strange sounds at the end.
My dub had mistakes like that in a few lines. I regenerated them, and then the audio was clean.
Step three, sync the audio with the video. This is the most important step.
Place each English line exactly where the Hindi line starts in the video.
If an English line is longer than its slot, speed it up a little, or shorten the translation.
But don't speed it up too much, or the voice will sound unnatural.
Then add the same background music and sound effects as the original video.
Because when a viewer picks the English track, the whole audio changes, not just the voice.
The final audio must be exactly as long as the video, with loudness close to the original.
Then export it as an audio file, like WAV or MP3. It shouldn't contain any video.
Step four, now let's upload the dub to YouTube.
Open YouTube Studio on a computer, and click Languages in the left menu.
On some accounts, this option may be called Subtitles.
Now choose the video you want to add the dub to.
Click Add language, and select English.
Now in the English row, click Add under Audio.
Select your English audio file, and let the upload finish.
After uploading, listen once in the preview to check the sync, then click Publish.
That's it. Your video now has an English audio track.
Step five, add an English title and description.
In the same English row, click Add under Title and description.
Write the title in English, using the words people actually search for in English.
In the description, add an English summary, chapters and important links.
This helps English viewers find your video in search, and see the title in their language.
Then click Publish.
Step six, add English subtitles too.
In the English row, click Add under Subtitles, and choose Upload file.
Select With timing, and upload your English SRT file.
Subtitles help people who watch without sound understand the video too.
And if you used an AI voice or a voice clone, answering Yes to the altered content question in the video details is the safe choice.
Now let's check that everything works.
Open your video, click the gear icon, choose English under Audio track, and listen to a few parts.
Check the start, the middle and the end, to make sure the voice matches the screen.
Now, some common mistakes to avoid.
First, an audio file that's a different length from the video.
Second, forgetting the background music in the English track, which makes the video feel empty.
Third, adding music or content that isn't in the original audio. YouTube may even remove that track.
And fourth, using the AI translation without reading it.
So let's recap.
First, check that your channel has multi-language audio, then translate the script.
Then make the English voice, sync it with the video, and build the final audio with music.
After that, add the English audio, title, description and subtitles in YouTube Studio.
That's all it takes for one video to reach viewers in two languages.
