4. DOCUMENTARY NARRATIVE (the channel's storytelling style)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Who we are:
  A Hindi documentary channel about the nights that changed history: disasters, tragedies and the people at the centre of them.

How every film is told:
  - Start with the night itself. The first minute puts the viewer inside the event: the date, the place, the silence before it happens.
  - Then the "why". What went wrong, step by step, in simple Hindi, without jargon.
  - The human story. Real people: the worker on shift, the station master, the doctor, the mother. Their names are used when they are known.
  - The aftermath. The toll, official figures and other estimates, always with the source made clear.
  - Today. What is left now: the place, the law, the people still fighting.
  - End on memory, not fear. Every film closes with a line on why we must remember.

Voice and tone:
  Calm, serious and respectful. No shouting, no exaggeration, no fake drama. Facts first; where numbers differ, say so.

Look:
  Dark, cinematic CGI reconstructions, warm amber light, and white captions with the date and place. Original music. Hindi narration with English subtitles.

Format:
  10–15 minute films in 1-minute chapters, with chapter timestamps in every description.

Ideas for the next films:
  - Titanic, 1912
  - The Partition of 1947: one train's story
  - The Uphaar cinema fire, Delhi, 1997
  - Fukushima, 2011
  - The Morbi bridge collapse, 2022
  - The Kedarnath floods, 2013
